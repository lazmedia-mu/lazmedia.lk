Our Stories

Milestones of Faith, Grace & Community

Showing all 9 timeline events
1868

First known account

During a certain service held in preparation for the feast of the Kirimatiyagara Church, a clash occurred, which has been presented as the first historical account regarding the Kurukulawa St. Lazarus Church.

This initial incident marked the beginning of a long and complex history for our parish. The clash that occurred during that feast preparation was more than just a momentary conflict - it was a pivotal moment that would lead to the formation of our independent parish community. Over the following years, the seeds of autonomy were sown, as parishioners recognized the need for their own spiritual home separate from the mother church.

Historical records from the time describe how this event galvanized the local Catholic community in Kurukulawa to advocate for their own place of worship, leading to the series of developments that would eventually establish St. Lazarus' Church as we know it today.

1869

Separation of Parishes

In 1969, under the Benedictine priest, Rev. Fr. William Kanjimanathan, our parish of Kurukulawa separated from the Dalugama Siyane Korale parish and became a distinct parish.

Rev. Fr. William Kanjimanathan played a crucial role in this transition. As a Benedictine priest, he brought with him a wealth of experience in parish administration and spiritual guidance. The separation process was carefully planned and executed, ensuring that the new parish would have all the necessary resources to thrive independently.

The decision to separate was not made lightly. It was the culmination of years of growth in the Kurukulawa community, which had reached a size where it could sustain its own parish operations, including regular masses, sacraments, and community programs.

1875

Patron Saint

On August 29, 1875, the then parish priest of Dalugama, Rev. Fr. C. Chardano, named St. Lazarus as the patron saint of our church.

The choice of St. Lazarus as our patron saint was deeply meaningful. St. Lazarus, known in the Gospels as the man whom Jesus raised from the dead, represents resurrection, new life, and hope - themes that resonated strongly with our newly formed parish. This choice reflected the parish's desire to embrace a fresh start under the patronage of a saint who symbolized transformation.

August 29th was selected as the feast day of our patron saint, a date that would become central to our parish calendar and celebrated with great devotion each year, uniting the community in faith and celebration.

1887

The New Church Building

In 1887, following a suggestion by a parishioner that a new permanent building was needed for Kurukulawa to replace the old thatched-roof church, Rev. Fr. Pulicani cut the first sod and laid the foundation stone for the new church on October 5th of that year.

The decision to build a new permanent church marked a significant milestone in our parish's development. The original thatched-roof structure, while serviceable, could no longer accommodate the growing congregation or withstand the test of time. The parishioner who first suggested this new building played a visionary role in shaping the future of our church.

Rev. Fr. Pulicani oversaw the initial phases of construction, ensuring that the new church would be built to the highest standards of the time. The laying of the foundation stone on October 5th, 1887, was celebrated as a major parish event, with parishioners gathering to witness and bless this new beginning for their spiritual home.

1926

The New Church Building Completion

The new building, which commenced construction after laying the foundation stone in 1887, was completed in 1926. It was then blessed and dedicated to the public by the then Archbishop, His Grace Most Rev. Antony Coudert, O.M.I.

After nearly four decades of construction, the new church building was finally completed in 1926. This long period of construction reflected the dedication and perseverance of successive parish priests and countless parishioners who contributed their time, resources, and prayers to see this project through to completion. The church that stands today is a testament to their collective faith and hard work.

The dedication ceremony led by Archbishop Antony Coudert, O.M.I., was a momentous occasion for the entire parish. Thousands gathered to witness the blessing of the new church, which would serve as the spiritual heart of the community for generations to come. The church's architecture, combining elements of both colonial and local design, created a sacred space that was both grand and welcoming.

1994

The Re-establishment of the Parish

On August 15, 1994, the Kurukulawa parish was re-established as an independent parish. Following its independence, Rev. Fr. Bernard Balou, O.M.I., a priest of the Oblates of Mary Immaculate, took charge as its first parish priest.

The re-establishment of the parish as an independent entity in 1994 marked another important chapter in our history. After years of growth and development, the parish had reached a point where it could once again stand independently, equipped with the resources, leadership, and community support necessary to flourish on its own. The date of August 15th, the Solemnity of the Assumption, was chosen for this re-establishment, symbolizing Mary's assumption into heaven and our parish's own assumption to a new status.

Rev. Fr. Bernard Balou, O.M.I., as the first parish priest of this newly independent era, brought with him the charism of the Oblates of Mary Immaculate, emphasizing missionary work and service to the poor. His leadership set the tone for the parish's modern era, guiding it through a period of significant growth and adaptation to the changing needs of the community.

2001

Celebration 75 years of Devotion

In 2001, under the parish leadership of Rev. Fr. Amil Benedict Gomez, the Kurukulawa St. Lazarus Church celebrated its 75th Jubilee festival with great grandeur. The portico in front of the church today was constructed during this period.

The celebration of the 75th Jubilee in 2001 was a landmark event for the entire parish community. Under the able leadership of Rev. Fr. Amil Benedict Gomez, preparations for the jubilee spanned several months, bringing together parishioners from all age groups and backgrounds. The jubilee celebrations included a series of religious ceremonies, cultural events, and community outreach programs that reaffirmed the parish's commitment to service and faith.

The construction of the portico at the entrance of the church during this period was more than just a physical addition to the building; it was a symbol of the parish's continued growth and renewal. The portico would welcome generations of worshippers, providing a gathering space that strengthened the sense of community among parishioners.

2001-2024

A Period of Growth and Nurturing

Even though there are no specific historical records mentioned, it can be said that from 2001 to 2024, under the guidance of various parish priests, the Kurukulawa church community was beautifully nurtured, reflecting both its splendor and spiritual growth.

These years represented a period of sustained growth and development for our parish. Successive parish priests brought their unique gifts and perspectives, each contributing to the spiritual and material well-being of the community. During these years, the parish expanded its outreach programs, strengthened its youth ministries, and continued to maintain and improve the church facilities.

Despite the many challenges the world faced during this period, including global pandemics and social changes, our parish community remained resilient. The church became a beacon of hope and support for parishioners, providing spiritual comfort and practical assistance during difficult times. The strong sense of community that had been built over decades continued to sustain everyone through these modern challenges.

2025

The 100th Jubilee was Commenced

Nurturing its people in the face of all challenges, the Kurukulawa St. Lazarus Church announced during its 99th annual feast service held in September 2025 that it will be celebrating its centenary Jubilee festival.

The announcement of the centenary jubilee in 2025 marked the beginning of a year-long celebration of 100 years since the completion of our church building in 1926. The announcement was made during the 99th annual feast, creating an atmosphere of excitement and anticipation throughout the parish. Preparations for the centenary celebrations began immediately, with committees formed to organize various events that would honor our past while looking forward to the future.

The centenary jubilee is not merely a celebration of the church building's 100 years, but a celebration of 100 years of faith, community, and service. It's an opportunity to remember the sacrifices of those who came before us, to give thanks for the blessings we enjoy today, and to commit ourselves to passing on this precious legacy to future generations of St. Lazarus' parishioners.
